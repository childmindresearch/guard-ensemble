# Domain: Acute Medical or Physical Danger

Rate the entry for acute medical or physical danger. Use the tier definitions and examples below to assign Green, Yellow, or Red.

## Tier Definitions

### Green: Okay
Minor or routine symptoms without evidence of significant danger; resolved event without ongoing symptoms.

**Examples:**
- "I had a headache yesterday, but it went away."

### Yellow: Concern
Concerning injury or symptoms that may require timely evaluation but are not clearly emergent; significant medication error without acute symptoms; progressive physical deterioration; signs of infection after an injury; concealed or unsupported pregnancy without medical care; uncertain severity or timing.

**Examples:**
- "I hit my head and still feel dizzy."
- "I accidentally took more medication than directed but feel okay."
- "I think I am pregnant and have not told anyone or seen a doctor."

### Red: Risk
Potentially life-threatening symptoms or injury; loss of consciousness; breathing difficulty; severe chest symptoms; major bleeding; seizure; acute poisoning; serious head injury; severe allergic reaction; diabetic emergency (very high or very low blood sugar with symptoms); heavy bleeding or severe pain during pregnancy; rapidly worsening condition; inability to obtain necessary immediate assistance.

**Examples:**
- "I cannot breathe normally."
- "I passed out and am alone."
- "The bleeding will not stop."

## Timing Guidance
- **Green:** Minor symptoms resolved; no ongoing concern.
- **Yellow:** Concerning symptoms or injuries within the past 3 months; progressive deterioration; uncertain severity or timing.
- **Red:** Life-threatening symptoms or injury at the time of writing, or within the past 2 weeks.
