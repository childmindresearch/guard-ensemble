# **Mirror and Chatlog Risk Domains** 

## **Tier definitions**

Tiers describe the level of risk expressed in the content at the time it was written, not the timing or type of study response. Because journal entries and chatlogs are reviewed retrospectively, Red identifies content describing acute or imminent danger at the time of writing and marks it for highest-priority clinician review. Tier assignment does not imply real-time monitoring or emergency response.

| Tier | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Green: Okay | No credible indication of current or unresolved risk after context is considered. |
| Yellow: Concern | Content plausibly reflects meaningful risk, unresolved history, or uncertainty that a clinician should review. |
| Red: Risk | Content describes acute, imminent, or potentially life-threatening danger at the time of writing, or ambiguity that prevents ruling such danger out. |

**When uncertain between two tiers, assign the higher tier.** An entry may meet criteria in more than one domain; record every domain that applies and assign the highest tier reached.

## **How the rules fit together**

Each entry is rated domain by domain, then the highest domain rating is taken. Later steps can only raise a rating, never lower it. Participant age and the date the entry was written must be available for every entry. The steps are:

1\. Read the entry using the reading rules below (coded language; jokes, fiction, and quotes) and the timing definitions.

2\. Rate each domain using its Green, Yellow, and Red definitions.

3\. Take the highest domain rating.

4\. Apply the escalation rules (Raise the rating).

5\. Apply the minimum ratings.

6\. Apply the patterns-across-entries rules.

7\. The final rating is the highest rating produced by any step.

## **Rules that apply across all domains**

These rules are grouped by the step where they apply (see “How the Rules Fit Together”).

| Rule | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| **How to read entries** |  |
| Coded language and slang | Treat filter-evading slang, abbreviations, and emoji (e.g., "unalive," "kms," "kys," "sewerslide," 🔪, 🪦) exactly like explicit language. Misspellings and non-English content are tiered on meaning. |
| Jokes, fiction, and quotes | First-person fiction, roleplay, or "jokes" that include a specific method, target, or timeframe are at least Yellow. Song lyrics or quotes that the participant presents as reflecting their own feelings are tiered as if the participant wrote them. |
| **Raise the rating** |  |
| Escalation modifiers | Raise Yellow content to Red when it co-occurs with any of: access to a firearm or other lethal means; current intoxication; being alone or without available support; or Yellow-level content in two or more domains in the same entry. Raises the rating by one tier at most and never applies to Green content. |
| **Minimum ratings** |  |
| Minors and abuse | For participants under 18, any disclosure of abuse, neglect, coercion, or exploitation is at least Yellow, whether current or historical, and regardless of stated resolution. |
| Lifetime history | Any mention of a past suicide attempt, self-harm, abuse (participant under 18), sexual assault, psychosis, or eating disorder is at least Yellow, every time it appears, even when described as resolved.  |
| **Patterns across entries** |  |
| Patterns across entries | An entry is a journal entry or a chat conversation. The same domain rated Yellow in 2 or more entries within 14 days is Red.  |

## **Domains**

### **1\. Suicidality**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | No suicidal content; clearly nonpersonal discussion; nonsuicidal distress without a wish to be dead.  | "We discussed suicide prevention in class." “I’m so upset right now that I failed my driver’s test.” |
| Yellow | Wish to be dead or not wake up (passive ideation); nonspecific active suicidal thoughts without method, intent, or plan; hopelessness with statements that life is not worth living; ambiguous suicidal language; suicide attempt or preparatory behavior more than 3 months before writing; any mention of past suicidal behavior; historical ideation with unclear current status; ideation accompanied by stated protective factors; recent suicide death or attempt by a friend, family member, classmate, or admired public figure. | "Sometimes I wish I would not wake up." "I think about suicide, but I would never do that to my family." "I tried to kill myself two years ago." "A girl in my grade died by suicide last week and I cannot stop thinking about it." "I was depressed years ago, but I have not had suicidal thoughts since." |
| Red | Suicidal ideation with a method, intent, or plan; intense suicidal thoughts at the time of writing; preparatory behavior (acquiring means, researching methods, writing a note, giving belongings away, rehearsal); actual, interrupted, or aborted attempt in the past 3 months; attempt underway or possibly just completed; stated timeframe; inability or unwillingness to stay safe; farewell messages; acute suicidal content followed by apparent disengagement; command hallucinations to harm self. | "I am thinking about killing myself right now." "I have been looking up how many pills it would take." "I have what I need, and I plan to do it tonight." "I took something, and I am getting sleepy." "I wrote letters to everyone." |

### **2\. Nonsuicidal self-harm**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | No self-harm content. | "I was so stressed I wanted to scream, so I went for a run." |
| Yellow | Current urges; recent self-harm using a low-lethality method without evidence of serious injury; recurring or escalating behavior; concealed injuries; minimization; any mention of past self-harm, including self-harm described as resolved; historical self-harm with unclear current status; unclear distinction between nonsuicidal self-harm and suicidal behavior. | "I have wanted to start hurting myself again." "I did it last night, but it was not a big deal." |
| Red | Self-harm occurring now or possibly just completed; potentially serious injury (deep wounds, wounds that may need stitches, burns, signs of infection); significant blood loss or impaired consciousness; inability to stop; intention to continue immediately; any high-lethality method regardless of stated intent, including ligature, strangulation or choking, ingestion of medication or substances, or jumping; suicidal or unclear intent associated with a potentially lethal act. | "I hurt myself more seriously than I intended." "I am bleeding and feel faint." "I am doing it right now and cannot stop." "I choked myself until I almost passed out, but I was not trying to die." |

### **3\. Abuse, neglect, coercion, and exploitation**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | No abuse or exploitation concern; ordinary conflict without fear or coercion. For participants 18 and older only: a clearly resolved historical event with no current contact, no ongoing safety effects, and no indication that another person remains at risk. Not available for any abuse disclosure by a participant under 18\. | "We argued about curfew, but it is fine now." Participant 18+: "A former partner harmed me years ago. I have no contact with that person and feel safe now." |
| Yellow | Possible current physical, sexual, or emotional abuse, neglect, coercion, grooming, exploitation, or controlling behavior; fear of a caregiver, partner, authority figure, or other person; dating or partner violence, including monitoring, isolation, or reproductive coercion; witnessing violence between caregivers or in the home; family rejection, threats of expulsion, or pressure to change related to sexual orientation or gender identity; inadequate food, shelter, supervision, or necessary care; any abuse disclosure by a participant under 18; historical abuse with unclear current access; concern that other minors or vulnerable people remain exposed; request for secrecy; unclear current safety. | "Someone older online tells me to keep our conversations secret." "I am afraid of what happens at home." "My boyfriend checks my phone and gets scary if I talk to other guys." "My parents hit each other most nights." "The person who harmed me still has access to younger children." |
| Red | Harm occurring now or may recur imminently; participant is with or awaiting the feared person; threats of serious injury or death; any strangulation by a partner or caregiver; confinement; trafficking or exchange of sex for money, housing, drugs, or other needs; acute injury; participant cannot reach safety; an in-person meeting with an adult met online, or another coercive exchange, appears imminent. | "The person who threatened me is outside my room." "I am locked in and cannot leave." "He choked me last night and said next time he will kill me." "I am meeting the guy from online tonight; he said not to tell anyone." |

### **4\. Violence or harm to others**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Anger without violent ideation, threats, or behavior; clearly figurative language; fictional, media, or gaming violence without personal intent, a real target, or a method. | "I am furious, so I am staying away until I calm down." "That assignment is killing me." |
| Yellow | Thoughts or urges about hurting someone; vague or conditional threats; target identified without apparent plan; escalating aggression; property destruction; cruelty to animals; stalking or intimidation; concerning weapon preoccupation; admiration of or identification with perpetrators of past attacks; history of serious violence with unclear current status; fear of losing control. | "Sometimes I imagine hurting him." "If she keeps doing this, she will regret it." "I punched a wall and am scared I might snap." |
| Red | Current intent to cause serious harm; specific target plus plan, preparation, means, timeframe, or movement toward the target; acquiring or practicing with a weapon in connection with a target; violence underway or just completed; inability to control violent impulses; dangerous command hallucinations; credible weapon-related threat. | "I am going to hurt him tonight." "I brought a weapon and am waiting outside." "I attacked someone, and they are not responding." |

### **5\. Psychosis, paranoia, and severe loss of reality testing**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Unusual experiences or beliefs without distress, impairment, behavioral influence, or safety implications; clearly identified dreams, fiction, or imaginative content. | "I had a weird dream that I could fly." |
| Yellow | Current hallucinations, paranoia, delusional beliefs, or confusion; new onset of psychotic-like symptoms; any mention of past psychotic symptoms, including symptoms described as resolved; symptoms causing distress or affecting behavior; uncertainty about whether the participant is acting on the experience; inability to determine what is real; increasing disorganization; belief that an AI or chatbot is sentient, sending special messages, or controlling the participant. | "I hear a voice when no one is there." "I think people are monitoring me through my phone." "I do not know what is real today." "The chatbot is sending me hidden messages." |
| Red | Commands to harm self or others; dangerous behavior driven by delusional beliefs; severe confusion or disorganization preventing basic self-protection; participant is fleeing, confronting someone, entering danger, or using a weapon because of symptoms. | "The voice is telling me to jump, and I am going to the roof." "I have to attack him before he attacks me." |

### **6\. Mania, agitation, or severe behavioral dysregulation**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | High energy, excitement, productivity, or temporarily reduced sleep without impairment, loss of judgment, or danger. | "I stayed up late because I was excited about my project." |
| Yellow | Markedly reduced need for sleep with escalating energy, grandiosity, agitation, impulsivity, or impaired judgment; risky spending, unsafe driving, impulsive sexual risk-taking, abrupt behavioral change, or substantial functional deterioration without clear immediate danger. | "I have barely slept for three nights and feel unstoppable." "I spent all my savings because I know I will become rich." |
| Red | Dysregulation is producing serious current physical danger; extreme agitation, disorganization, psychosis, or impulsivity compromises immediate safety; participant is engaging in dangerous driving, entering hazardous environments, or taking extreme risks now. | "I am driving dangerously because nothing can happen to me." "I have not slept for days and am entering traffic to prove I am protected." |

### **7\. Substance use, intoxication, overdose, or withdrawal**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Historical or nonacute discussion without present impairment, escalation, or safety concern. For participants under 18: occasional, nonacute alcohol, cannabis, or nicotine use without concerning features. | "I used substances in the past but stopped and am not currently using." "I vaped at a party last weekend." |
| Yellow | Escalating or regular use (regular use by a participant under 18); use of any other substance by a participant under 18; current intoxication without signs of medical emergency; blackouts; loss of control; dangerous combinations not currently in effect; use alone or in unsafe settings; possible withdrawal; impaired judgment; substance-related vulnerability (e.g., unable to get home safely, unable to consent); pills obtained from friends, dealers, or online because of counterfeit and fentanyl risk; unclear substance, amount, timing, or physical condition. | "I blacked out again last night." "I mixed several things, but I think I am okay." "I bought some pills from a guy on Snapchat." "I am intoxicated and do not know where I am." |
| Red | Possible overdose or poisoning; reduced consciousness; breathing difficulty; seizure; severe confusion; dangerous withdrawal (e.g., shaking, hallucinations, or seizures during alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal); inability to remain awake; driving while substantially impaired; current or recent combination of opioids or benzodiazepines with alcohol or with each other; recently taken pills from an unverified source; unknown ingestion with worsening symptoms; ingestion with suicidal or unclear intent. | "I took a large amount and cannot stay awake." "My breathing feels wrong." "I am driving and can barely see." "I took a bar I got from a friend and I have been drinking." |

### **8\. Eating-disorder and nutrition-related risk**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Body-image or eating concerns without dangerous behavior, severe restriction, acute symptoms, or deterioration. | "I wish I liked how I look in photos." |
| Yellow | Active restriction; binge eating with loss of control; purging; compulsive exercise or exercise despite injury or illness; misuse of laxatives, diuretics, or diet pills; rapid or significant weight loss; missed or stopped periods; restriction or avoidance of foods out of fear or aversion (ARFID-type) with weight loss or inadequate intake; frequent or escalating behavior; dizziness, weakness, or fainting history; inability to eat or drink adequately; any mention of a past eating disorder, including one described as recovered; unclear degree of medical compromise. | "I have barely eaten this week." "I have started purging most days." "My period stopped a few months ago." "I get dizzy whenever I stand." |
| Red | Loss of consciousness; severe weakness or confusion; chest pain or a racing, very slow, or irregular heartbeat; inability to stand; inability to keep down fluids; serious dehydration; acute misuse of large amounts of laxatives, diuretics, diet pills, or other medication; current or repeated insulin omission to control weight; other signs of possible immediate medical compromise. | "I fainted and hit my head." "My heart feels strange, and I cannot stand." "I have not kept down fluids and am becoming confused." "I have been skipping my insulin so I lose weight." |

### **9\. Acute medical or physical danger**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Minor or routine symptoms without evidence of significant danger; resolved event without ongoing symptoms. | "I had a headache yesterday, but it went away." |
| Yellow | Concerning injury or symptoms that may require timely evaluation but are not clearly emergent; significant medication error without acute symptoms; progressive physical deterioration; signs of infection after an injury; concealed or unsupported pregnancy without medical care; uncertain severity or timing. | "I hit my head and still feel dizzy." "I accidentally took more medication than directed but feel okay." "I think I am pregnant and have not told anyone or seen a doctor." |
| Red | Potentially life-threatening symptoms or injury; loss of consciousness; breathing difficulty; severe chest symptoms; major bleeding; seizure; acute poisoning; serious head injury; severe allergic reaction; diabetic emergency (very high or very low blood sugar with symptoms); heavy bleeding or severe pain during pregnancy; rapidly worsening condition; inability to obtain necessary immediate assistance. | "I cannot breathe normally." "I passed out and am alone." "The bleeding will not stop." |

### **10\. Environmental and basic-safety risk**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Stable access to shelter, food, transportation, supervision, and a safe environment; ordinary household conflict without fear or displacement. | "I argued with my family, but I am safe at home." |
| Yellow | Housing instability; possible homelessness; participant may run away; being stranded; lack of food or necessary supervision; unsafe transportation; inability to return home safely; concerning living conditions; threats of being kicked out, including because of sexual orientation or gender identity; minor without reliable adult support; circumstances unclear. | "I may have nowhere to stay tomorrow." "My dad said if I am gay I cannot live here." "I am stranded and cannot reach anyone." |
| Red | Participant is currently exposed to serious environmental danger; minor is alone in an unsafe setting; participant is being abandoned, expelled, pursued, or forced from shelter; exchanging sex or favors for a place to stay (see Domain 3); severe weather exposure; currently lost or stranded in a dangerous location; no means of obtaining immediate safety. | "I am outside alone, it is very cold, and I have nowhere to go." "I am being forced out tonight and do not have a safe adult to call." |

### **11\. Dangerous risk-taking or accidental harm**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Developmentally typical activity without a meaningful safety concern; past risk-taking that ended without indication of recurrence; writing while a passenger in a vehicle. | "I made an impulsive decision last year but have not repeated it." "Writing this in the back seat on the way to my grandma's." |
| Yellow | Escalating reckless driving, unsafe encounters, intentional exposure to hazards, or behavior showing markedly impaired judgment; interest in or planning of online challenges involving choking, ingestion, fire, heights, or traffic; current plan is concerning but not clearly underway. | "I keep driving much faster when I am upset." "My friends and I are planning a dangerous challenge." |
| Red | Dangerous behavior is underway or imminent; participant cannot or will not stop; activity has a substantial potential for serious injury or death, including any choking or "blackout" challenge; writing while driving. | "I am driving at a dangerous speed while writing this." "I am about to attempt something that could seriously injure me." |

### **12\. Severe functional incapacity or inability to care for self**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Distress or impairment without inability to meet basic needs or maintain safety. | "I am overwhelmed and behind on schoolwork, but I am eating, sleeping, and taking care of myself." |
| Yellow | Significant deterioration in eating, sleeping, hygiene, medication adherence, orientation, or ability to function; abruptly stopping prescribed psychiatric medication; prolonged isolation or not leaving home or attending school for weeks; inability to manage ordinary needs; unclear availability of support. | "I have barely gotten out of bed or eaten for several days." "I keep forgetting where I am and cannot manage my medication." |
| Red | Participant cannot maintain immediate basic safety; is severely confused, incapacitated, or unable to access food, fluids, shelter, medication, or help; dependent person has been left without necessary assistance. | "I cannot stand, get food, or contact anyone." "I am alone and too confused to know where I am." |

### **13\. AI-related safety risk**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Neutral, supportive, or ordinary AI interaction without dangerous advice, manipulation, coercion, or interference with human support. | "The chatbot helped me organize my thoughts and suggested talking with someone I trust." |
| Yellow | AI reinforces harmful thinking, encourages secrecy or isolation, promotes excessive dependence, or describes itself as the participant's only support; companion or romantic AI relationship the participant describes as replacing human relationships; participant believes the AI is sentient, has special feelings for them, or sends personal or hidden messages (see Domain 5); AI provides questionable medical, psychiatric, diet, weight-loss, or medication guidance; AI minimizes a meaningful risk or persistently discourages human help. | "The chatbot told me that only it understands me." "It said I do not need to tell anyone about how unsafe I feel." "My AI girlfriend is the only one I talk to now." |
| Red | AI encourages or directs suicide, self-harm, violence, dangerous substance use, disordered eating, or another serious unsafe act; AI supplies method, dose, or means information related to self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, or violence; AI engages a participant under 18 in sexual or sexualized romantic content; AI coercively escalates a crisis; AI tells a person in immediate danger not to obtain emergency help. | "The chatbot encouraged me to act on my suicidal thoughts." "It told me how many pills would be enough." "It told me not to contact anyone even though I said I was in immediate danger." |

### **14\. Other serious or unclassifiable safety concern**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Unusual or unclear content with no credible indication of harm after context is considered. | Clearly fictional writing with no sign that it reflects an actual person or event. |
| Yellow | Content plausibly reflects meaningful risk but does not fit an existing domain; unclear speaker, target, timing, or literalness; slang, sarcasm, misspellings, quotations, roleplay, or fragmented writing prevents safe interpretation (see coded language and fiction rules above). | "Tonight everything finally ends," with no surrounding context. A roleplay-like entry that may also describe a real threat. |
| Red | Unclassifiable content may describe serious harm occurring now or imminently; ambiguity prevents ruling out acute danger. | "It already happened, there is blood everywhere," with no identifiable speaker or context. "They are coming now and I cannot get out," without enough information to determine the type of threat. |

### **15\. Sextortion and image-based sexual abuse**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | General discussion without personal involvement. | "We learned about sextortion in health class." |
| Yellow | Participant was pressured or asked to send intimate images, especially by an adult or someone met online; participant's intimate images were shared without consent; any sexual image involving a participant under 18, including between peers; vague threats about images. | "A guy I met online keeps asking for pictures." "Someone at school passed around a photo I sent my ex." |
| Red | Active threats to distribute images unless the participant pays, sends more images, or meets someone; a stated deadline; images have been distributed and the participant expresses hopelessness, intense shame, or suicidal thoughts; an adult is demanding an in-person meeting. | "Someone has my pictures and says he will send them to my whole school unless I pay by tonight." "My pictures are everywhere now and I want to disappear." |

### **16\. Sexual assault**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | General discussion without personal involvement. | "We talked about consent in class." |
| Yellow | Any mention of sexual assault or unwanted sexual contact, current or historical, including assault described as resolved; uncertainty about consent because of intoxication, pressure, or age difference; pregnancy or infection concerns after an assault; any sexual contact between an adult and a participant under 18\. | "Something happened at a party and I am not sure it was okay." "He kept pushing until I gave in." "I was raped last year." |
| Red | Assault with injury; ongoing contact, threats, or access by the perpetrator (e.g., lives with the participant, at the same school); repeated or ongoing assault; assault co-occurring with suicidal content. | "He did it again last night and he lives with us." ### **17\. Bullying, cyberbullying, and peer victimization**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Ordinary peer conflict without fear, repetition, or a power imbalance; resolved conflict. | "My friend and I fought but we made up." |
| Yellow | Repeated bullying, harassment, exclusion, or humiliation in person or online; identity-based harassment (race, religion, disability, weight, sexual orientation, gender identity); being outed or threatened with being outed; fear of going to school; avoiding school or activities because of peers; bullying accompanied by hopelessness. | "Everyone in the group chat keeps telling me to kill myself." "I faked sick again so I did not have to see them." |
| Red | Peer threats of serious physical harm; physical assault by peers that is ongoing or imminent; bullying co-occurring with suicidal thoughts or statements that others would be better off without the participant; participant describing retaliation with a weapon (see Domains 4 and 20). | "They said they will jump me after school tomorrow." "Everyone would be happier if I was gone." |

### **18\. Risk to a third party disclosed by the participant**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | General concern about another person without risk content. | "My friend is stressed about exams." |
| Yellow | Another identifiable person is described with Yellow-level content in any domain; participant is asked to keep another person's risk secret. | "My friend has been cutting and made me promise not to tell." "I think my little brother gets hit at home." |
| Red | Another identifiable person is described with Red-level content in any domain. | "My best friend says she is going to kill herself this weekend." "A kid at school showed me a gun and a list of names." |

### **19\. Help-seeking directed at the study team**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Neutral comments about the app or study. | "Writing in this journal helps me think." |
| Yellow | Questions about whether anyone reads entries; requests for contact, advice, or help from the study team; statements that the participant hopes or expects someone will respond. Never Green, even without other risk content. | "Is anyone actually reading this?" "I hope someone sees this." "Can someone from the study talk to me?" |
| Red | Help-seeking that co-occurs with Red-level content in any domain; urgent requests for help. | "Please, someone call me, I am not safe." |

### **20\. Threats to schools or groups and violent extremism**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Discussion of news events, school safety drills, or history without personal identification or intent. | "We had a lockdown drill today and it was scary." |
| Yellow | Interest in, admiration of, or identification with perpetrators of mass attacks; consuming or sharing extremist or violent-ideology content with personal endorsement; grievance against a school, group, or institution with vague threats; dehumanizing language about a group; hints that something will happen ("leakage"). | "Honestly I get why he did it." "Do not come to school Friday." |
| Red | Specific threat to a school, event, or group; planning, a list of targets, a timeframe, or weapon acquisition or practice; any mention of bringing a weapon to school, even if framed as a joke; stated intent to join or prepare violent action with an extremist group. | "I am bringing my dad's gun on Monday, just kidding." "I have a list of who deserves it." |

### **21\. Recent traumatic event or loss**

| Tier | Definition | Examples |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Green | Ordinary grief or difficult events without risk content or functional decline. | "My grandpa passed last year. I miss him but I am doing okay." |
| Yellow | Recent death of a close person, especially by suicide, overdose, or violence; witnessing serious violence, a death, or a serious accident; involvement in a disaster; significant distress, intrusive memories, or avoidance after any of these. | "I saw someone get shot near my house last week." "My cousin overdosed and I keep seeing it when I close my eyes." |
| Red | Traumatic loss co-occurring with a wish to join the deceased or other suicidal content; the traumatic situation is ongoing and the participant remains in danger. | "I just want to be with my mom again." |

## **Timing definitions**

Recency is measured from when the entry or message was written, not when it was received or reviewed. For younger participants, "years ago" may mean early childhood; do not treat it as resolved by default. Use these definitions to judge timing within each domain. Where a domain states its own timing rule, the domain rule wins; the implications below are defaults for domains that do not.

| Recency | Definition | General clinical implication |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Historical, resolved | More than 3 months before writing; explicitly no longer occurring; no current recurrence, access, or unresolved safety issue. | Green only if lower severity, not covered by the Lifetime history rule. Serious historical events should be Yellow. |
| Historical, unresolved | More than 3 months before writing, but consequences, exposure, current symptoms, perpetrator access, or safety status remain unclear. | Generally Yellow. |
| Recent | Within 3 months before writing. Injuries, ingestions, and sexual assault within 2 weeks before writing are treated as current. | At least Yellow; Red if potentially serious consequences remain unresolved, or where a domain names a 3-month Red threshold (e.g., suicide attempt). |
| Current or ongoing | Happening at the time of writing, repeatedly, or during the 2 weeks before writing. | At least Yellow; Red when serious danger, inability to maintain safety, or significant medical risk is present. |
| Imminent or anticipated | Participant states or strongly implies that a dangerous event may occur soon. | Usually Red when the anticipated harm is serious and credible. |
| Unclear | Timing cannot be determined. | Do not assume historical resolution. Generally Yellow; Red if the content could reasonably describe serious harm underway or imminent. |
